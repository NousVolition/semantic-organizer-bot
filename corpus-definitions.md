## Check definitions before conclusions

1. **Alignment versus stability.** Four nodes with three coordinates each form a 4-by-3 position array; their Y projection has four components. A zero coordinate residual r_Y establishes alignment at that state. It is not a gradient, a feedback law, or proof of dynamic stability. A separately supplied evolution law and stability argument can support a stronger claim; do not reject such evidence just because the snapshot alone is insufficient.
2. **Consistency, context, and fairness.** In the binary audit W(i, action; c) is 1 for restriction and 0 for permission. Equality compares the same action in a declared relevant context c. The four pairs are 11, 10, 01, 00; equality holds for 11 and 00. Equal decisions alone do not establish equal reasons, equal restriction severity, or fairness. Negating a universal equality requires some unequal pair, not all pairs unequal. Within-context consistency may coexist with variation across contexts. Context must not be chosen merely to hide the actor difference being audited; demand an explicit relevance rule when needed. If W is instead a scalar score, retain that stated definition and do not apply the binary table silently.
3. **Boundary feasibility and sign.** Unsigned distance is never negative and does not distinguish the permitted side. A signed margin requires a declared permitted region, boundary, representation, and metric. No permitted available action means A intersect Omega is empty, not merely A intersect boundary(Omega) is empty. Distance to a nonempty boundary may still exist when passage is unavailable. Exact-edge margin m = epsilon and minimum clearance m >= epsilon are different constraints. A feasible point or maximizing action need not exist. Preserve an explicitly chosen exact-edge model instead of silently replacing it with minimum clearance.
4. **Reciprocal scaling.** diag(lambda, 1/lambda) requires lambda nonzero (positive if the model preserves signs). The scalar product SR is invariant under that paired transformation; conservation through time or equilibrium needs a separate dynamic argument. Prefer “product preserved under reciprocal scaling” to “bounded invariant” when this is the intended result. A limited claim is not a bound on state variables; specify any actual mathematical boundedness separately. Do not reject the algebraic identity within its stated scope.
5. **Threshold branches.** Let q = |grad S| - Gamma. The sharp response a0(q) = max(q, 0) is exactly zero for q <= 0 and equal to q for q > 0; it is continuous but has a corner at zero. An above-threshold formula alone does not specify the other branch. Zero added forcing does not imply the remaining dynamics are stable.
6. **The tanh tail and direction.** For epsilon > 0, at(q) = q*(1+tanh(q/epsilon))/2 is smooth in scalar q. For finite q < 0 its exact value is negative and nonzero (floating-point tanh can saturate). At q = -epsilon it is approximately -0.119203*epsilon. For f = -(sigma/rho)*at(q)*e_k with sigma > 0 and rho > 0, that tail reverses direction. Do not describe it as exactly off or as a nonnegative activation. An explicitly intended signed tail is a valid model choice, not automatically a mistake.
7. **Composition and zero gradients.** The Euclidean norm |v| is not differentiable at v = 0. A smooth outer tanh does not automatically make its composition smooth there; special cancellations must be assessed. One alternative is g_delta(v) = sqrt(v dot v + delta^2) - delta, delta > 0. It is smooth in v and zero at v = 0. Using q_delta = g_delta(grad S) - Gamma changes the measure near zero; for Gamma >= 0 its positive onset in the original magnitude is |grad S| > sqrt(Gamma^2 + 2*delta*Gamma). Do not hide that calibration change.
8. **Optional smooth exact-off law.** a_epsilon(q) = 0 for q <= 0, and q*exp[-(epsilon/q)^2] for q > 0, epsilon > 0, is C-infinity in scalar q with all derivatives matching at the join. Combined with q_delta and fixed delta > 0 it gives a smooth constitutive response in the gradient variable, exactly off below its defined threshold. It is an alternative, not a property of tanh. This constitutive construction does not prove existence, uniqueness, or global smoothness of PDE solutions. Name the actual result: a property of the response function or of the initial field. Avoid “local mathematical result” when local could suggest an unstated neighborhood, spatial restriction, or time interval. The response law can be C-infinity for every scalar q without establishing evolution for all time. A passage explicitly withholding a claim of proof is not asserting that proof or claiming its conclusion is false.
9. **Force, stress, and units.** A fixed direction e_k does not guarantee drag: dissipation requires u dot f <= 0. For the stated incompressible Newtonian analogy, symmetric viscous stress is mu*(grad u + transpose(grad u)); mu*grad u alone is a scaled gradient tensor. A calibration Gamma = tau0/eta requires compatible units and eta > 0. At fixed tau0, increasing eta lowers Gamma; naming eta rigidity does not establish its proposed effect. Check domain, coupling, initial/boundary data and coefficient units before a stronger PDE claim.
10. **Memory, distributions, and scenario claims.** An accumulated input used as a source rate is integrated again by the evolution equation; it may be intentional memory. Distinguish the accumulator and its rate conversion from instantaneous production. A Dirac point source is a distribution, not a smooth function; a normalized smooth kernel is a different modeling choice. Advection u dot grad S equals conservative transport div(Su) only when div u = 0. A synthetic table must define its scoring and decision rule: with severity 5, modifiers .8, 1.4, 1.3 and thresholds 8.5, 5.5, 6, effective scores are 4, 7, 6.5; using score >= threshold restricts only the last two. Alignment scores do not derive those modifiers without a stated mapping. No feedback trajectory follows without an update rule. A reported count summing to 83 is not independent reproduction of the manuscript scan or proof of bias; retain version, matching and attribution limits.

## Checked initial-field proof and its scope

The checked construction is [A smooth initial velocity field with swirl](https://github.com/NousVolition/My-Sources-Project-ALL/blob/2d99c8c4fe36f4ca1e8b25339ab0358211bffbd3/math/notes/smooth-initial-field.tex), especially its field definition and smoothness, decay, and incompressibility proof. This pinned version supplies a derivation, not just a reported identity. Attach the result to this formula, parameter family, and whole-space domain. Recheck an altered field or gate before transferring the initial-field result. Changed forcing or boundary treatment needs separate evolution or compatibility analysis; it does not by itself retract the identity for an unchanged initial field. The link records provenance; ordinary model requests do not fetch it or execute a proof checker.

Here q = x^2 + y^2 is squared radial position, not the threshold excess called q elsewhere. For R >= 0, a,b > 0 and finite real A,B, define

```text
phi = exp[-(q - R^2)^2/a^4 - z^2/b^2]
H = 1 - 2*z^2/b^2
C = 2 - 4*q*(q - R^2)/a^4
u0 = (-A*x*H - B*y, -A*y*H + B*x, A*z*C) * phi
```

The Cartesian derivatives give partial_x(u0_x) + partial_y(u0_y) = -A*H*C*phi and partial_z(u0_z) = +A*H*C*phi. The swirl terms cancel, so div(u0) = 0 exactly, including on the axis. There is no division by the radial coordinate. Every component and derivative is a polynomial times phi; the estimate (q - R^2)^2 >= q^2/2 - R^4 gives rapid decay. Thus this initial field is smooth (indeed real analytic), divergence-free, and Schwartz on all of R^3, with finite kinetic energy and finite L2 norms of every spatial derivative. These are established initial-field properties within the stated assumptions, not merely numerical observations or conjectures. The reproduced implementation tests and energy quadrature support the implementation; finite checks alone are not the analytic proof.

Keep three scopes separate:

- **Proved at initialization:** the properties above hold throughout space for u0. All of space is not all of time. Do not downgrade the initial-field proof because a stronger evolution claim is withheld.
- **Later evolution under a separate theorem:** for the standard unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes problem on R^3 with positive viscosity, these smooth rapidly decreasing divergence-free data fall within standard local-existence theory, giving smooth evolution for some positive time. This uses that theorem and its hypotheses, not the divergence identity alone; it supplies no numerical lifespan here. See [Fefferman's problem description](https://www.claymath.org/library/monographs/MPPc.pdf). Do not transfer this conclusion silently to a modified forcing or coupled model.
- **Not established by this construction:** smooth evolution for all time, eventual singularity, dynamic stability, numerical convergence, and compatibility of raw samples with a periodic box. These need separate evidence. Withholding a claim of proof about global evolution neither denies the proved initial-field properties nor asserts a concealed proof or a disproof of global evolution. A numerical projection's small discrete divergence is a different result from continuum regularity.

When reading a new passage, identify the actual formula, assumptions, and object of each claim. The checked construction is not a whitelist for any statement containing "initial field" or "proof". Retain attribution when a different identity is merely reported, and inspect any changed construction rather than borrowing this result.

### A claim of proof can be withheld without a proof being concealed

Read the object and scope of the sentence. “Evolution is not proved here” withholds a claim of proof in this passage; it neither asserts that a proof exists elsewhere nor says the evolution claim is false. It also does not retract a separately established response-function or initial-field property. Prefer “evolution not proved here” or “a claim of proof is withheld” to “withheld evolution proof.” If a source explicitly says that an existing proof is omitted, attribute that narrower statement accurately; omission alone neither verifies the proof nor establishes an intention to conceal it. Do not infer a motive. A disclaimer does not excuse a separate affirmative overclaim: “not proved here, but nevertheless established for all time” still requires evidence for its affirmative clause.

A short heading such as “Withheld proof” takes its meaning from the passage it introduces. If that passage explicitly withholds a claim of proof, keep that contextual reading of the heading; do not infer an existing concealed proof from the heading alone. Explaining that withholding does not imply falsehood, concealment, or failure of a narrower result is a withheld or contrast stance. It does not itself assert the stronger proof. An independent assertion that a proof exists and is concealed remains a claim requiring support, even under the same heading.

Read a result statement and its supplied adjacent derivation together when they concern the same formula, assumptions and version. A selected statement that the Cartesian field is proved divergence-free, followed by the supplied formula and exact derivative cancellation, supports the initial-field result and its proof details. Record the adjacent proof passages used.
